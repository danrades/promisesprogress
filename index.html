Promises and Progress

How far have the promises of freedom and equality been fulfilled?

Content note: This lesson addresses slavery, racism and inequality. We analyse history respectfully. Nobody has to share personal experiences or speak for a group.

Learning goals

  • Explain progress in law, politics and everyday life.
  • Understand Obama’s view that progress is real but incomplete.
  • Connect his argument to emancipation, Reconstruction, segregation and Civil Rights.
  • Support a short judgement with historical evidence.

1 · What counts as progress?

Sort each example into the best category. Some examples may fit more than one category; choose the strongest match.

Think–pair–share

Is a promise fulfilled when it is written into law, or only when people can experience it in everyday life?

2 · Obama on progress

At a 2015 commemoration of the 150th anniversary of the 13th Amendment, President Barack Obama described American progress as:

“hard-fought, hard-won, incomplete, but always possible.”

He also described abolition as “not a final step, but rather the first step” towards making equality real.

Context: political speech, not statistical evidence

Vocabulary

Quick check

1. What does “hard-won” mean?
2. Why call abolition a “first step”?

Read closely

Which words show progress? Which words show that progress remains incomplete?
